Choosing the right tool for the problem

The appropriate tool depends on the risk, context, cost, and standard of proof.

4 Jul 2026

QuestionWhich tool fits the risk and standard of proof?

TopicsAI · Decision-making · Verification

AI models are not interchangeable assistants. A fast, low-cost model is enough when the work is reversible: summarising notes, drafting rough copy, cleaning up text, or generating first-pass alternatives. The goal in those cases is momentum, not final judgment.

Stronger reasoning models become more useful when the work contains ambiguity, trade-offs, or hidden assumptions. Strategy questions, market-entry logic, operating-model choices, and stakeholder-sensitive recommendations need slower thinking because a fluent answer can still be directionally wrong.

Coding agents sit in a different category. They are most useful when they can inspect files, implement changes, run checks, and work through errors in context. Their value comes from the relationship between the model and the environment, not simply from a benchmark score.

Choosing by consequence

The choice should reflect risk, context size, cost, reversibility, and verification needs. The useful question is which model is appropriate for this decision, this constraint, and this standard of proof.

A sound system has room for more than one tool. Use a lighter tool when the output is easy to check and replace. Use a stronger one when the decision is ambiguous or expensive to reverse. Keep human judgment where the consequences require it.
