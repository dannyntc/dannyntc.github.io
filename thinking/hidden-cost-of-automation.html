What I lose when AI does the first thinking

What changes when a model gets the first word, before I have formed a view of my own.

6 Jul 2026

Working principleUse AI to improve work while keeping independent judgment active.

TopicsAI · Attention · Critical thinking

I use AI to remove friction, which is useful. I also notice that some friction is where my thinking develops. When I hand every blank page to a model, the output arrives faster, but I can skip the uncomfortable stage of forming a position, noticing gaps, and building confidence in an argument of my own.

I am especially cautious when the work involves hard reading, synthesis, or sitting with uncertainty. My concern is not that AI always makes those activities worse; it is that beginning with a polished answer can make it easier to stop before I have worked out what I think.

Think first, then use AI

A useful pattern for me is to think first, then use AI: form an initial view, ask the model to challenge it, compare alternatives, and decide what still needs human judgment. Used this way, AI becomes a sparring partner and accelerator, not a substitute for attention.

The point is not to protect every manual step. Some work is repetitive and should be made easier. The point is to notice which steps are carrying judgment or understanding, and to keep enough of that work in view that I can still explain and own the result.
