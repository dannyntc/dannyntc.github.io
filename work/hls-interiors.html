Work · HLS Interiors

Changing the basis of growth

Whether a low-margin model could support a better position in the market.

HLS InteriorsCorporate Strategy & OperationsMar 2025 – Jun 2026

FocusBusiness transformation

ContributionPositioning, pricing, growth systems, and operations

Evidence2× revenue growth · 30% margin improvement

The situation

HLS Interiors was operating in a low-margin, volume-driven segment. Taking on more work could increase activity without necessarily creating a better business. The economics made the limits visible: the business was difficult to scale while keeping the work profitable and consistent.

The question

The opportunity was not simply to acquire more customers. It was to reconsider the business model itself: the position in the market, the customers to prioritise, how work should be priced, and what the service offering needed to become.

That meant looking at unit economics, customer profitability, competitor positioning, and the operating requirements behind a more valuable service. A feasibility analysis pointed toward higher-value home-improvement services rather than simply pursuing more volume. The strategic question became: what would make the business more useful to the right customers, and what systems would make that promise deliverable?

The work

  • Conducted market and competitor research to understand the existing position and the opportunity to move toward higher-value household services.
  • Developed a pivot proposal covering positioning, target customers, scope-scaled pricing, and customer acquisition priorities.
  • Built customer acquisition, retention, and win-back pipelines, including intermediary outreach to more than 1,500 contacts and a partner pipeline of more than 100 relationships.
  • Established operating procedures, internal documentation, and structured checklists for consistent delivery and quality control.
  • Used AI-assisted tools to reduce administrative work and shorten customer outreach processes from weeks to days, while monitoring errors and handoffs.
  • Trained team members on the revised procedures and supported vendor and stakeholder decisions as the operating model took shape.

The result

2×Revenue growth following the business pivot.
30%Margin improvement following positioning and pricing changes.

The outcomes are presented as contributions following the pivot and related changes in positioning and pricing. The 30% figure refers to margin improvement, not percentage points.

What I took from it

One lesson from the work is that growth questions often begin as positioning questions. Before adding more activity, it helps to ask what the business is optimising for, which customers create healthy economics, and whether the operating system can support the promise being made.
