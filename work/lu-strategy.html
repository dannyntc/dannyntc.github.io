Work · Lu Strategy

Making fragmented operations usable

A system had to fit the way the team actually worked.

Lu Strategy Consulting GroupAssociate Consultant · Operations SupportApr 2021 – Sep 2024

FocusOperational transformation

ContributionDigitisation, standardisation, training, and adoption

Evidence300+ records · 40% less friction · 60% less manual work

The situation

Information at Lu Strategy was spread across paper records, informal tracking systems, and disconnected processes. The visible problem was administrative workload, but the deeper problem was that information was not centralised and routine work was not repeatable.

The question

A new tool alone would not solve the problem. The system had to match how the team actually worked, support a non-technical manager, and reduce the effort required to keep information current. The work therefore combined platform evaluation with process design, feedback, training, and a gradual shift away from paper.

The useful test was whether a teammate could find the information, understand the next step, and complete the task without relying on a private workaround.

The work

  • Evaluated more than 10 database and CRM solutions against the firm's requirements.
  • Implemented Airtable as a centralised database for more than 300 customer records, requirements, and appointments.
  • Migrated paper records into Google Workspace using scanning and OCR-assisted document digitisation.
  • Standardised templates, forms, and administrative procedures so recurring work followed a clearer sequence.
  • Gathered feedback from teammates and the manager, adjusted the system configuration, and delivered targeted training to support adoption.
  • Reviewed the new operating approach after implementation and continued to identify gaps and practical improvements.

The result

~40%Less administrative friction after moving paper-based records into shared digital systems.
~60%Less manual work through standardised templates, forms, and procedures.

The two figures describe different improvements and are intentionally kept separate: administrative friction after digitisation, and manual work after standardisation.

What I took from it

Operational change becomes real when the people doing the work can use it without fighting the system. A process map or database is only a starting point. The details that determine adoption are often smaller: naming, defaults, handoffs, training, and whether the new path feels easier than the old workaround.
